The Second Ship

Ethics and Leadership · Week of October 19, 2026 · 12–15 minute story, knowledge check, then 8–10 minute Decision Note. Publication is unassigned.

One focus: 3b.3

Objective: I can identify a need and compare two responses using relevant benefits and costs. Evidence: Decision Note prompt 3 and presentation notes compare two responses to the same need. Success: identify an affected group, a concrete benefit and cost for each response, and explain why one tradeoff is preferable.

Case and source

In 427 BCE Athens reconsidered its collective punishment of Mytilene. Thucydides contrasts Cleon’s case for firmness with Diodotus’s argument about Athenian interests. A second ship prevented the first order; later punishment still followed. The episode preserves that sequence. Its messengers, conversations, recommendations and scoring are invented.

Read Thucydides 3.36–50, especially 3.49–50. The speeches are Thucydides’ historical presentation, not recordings. Connection to earlier Ethics work: authority, reasons, consequences and the gap between a decision and its implementation. Current weekly reading/vocabulary alignment has not been verified; listed terms are episode concepts.

What the points mean

Your score measures success under the game’s stated priorities, not an objective measure of moral goodness.

Human lives, Athenian security, Justice and Public trust receive equal weight. Each choice adds 1–4 points to each category; seven choices create a 28-point scale per category and 112-point combined scale. No route achieves every maximum. Scores evaluate the game’s assumptions about recommendations, not actual lives saved or measured historical effects.

Replay can improve a total or change its balance. Ask whether a higher total conceals a serious cost. Challenge the equal weights, short-term emphasis on security, and omission of Mytilenean independence as a separate category. Simulation points never determine grades. Four knowledge questions remain separate: 25 points each, 75 required with review/retry.

Five-part Decision Note

Write about 80–120 words total, or five concise bullets. Use your own reasoning; you do not have to agree with the game’s scores.

  1. Choose: Which recommendation should your messenger make at one of the seven decisions?
  2. Explain: Give your strongest reason and one specific episode detail.
  3. Compare responses: What need is at stake? Compare your response with the strongest alternative, giving a benefit and a cost of each for affected people.
  4. State a principle: What general rule supports your judgement?
  5. Test it: What change of circumstance might make you reconsider that rule? Explain why, or defend the rule against the challenge.

Frames: “This detail matters because…”; “Both responses address…, but…”; “Someone could reasonably disagree because…”; “My rule becomes difficult when…”

Optional principle starters: “Leaders should reconsider decisions when…”; “Security may justify…, but not…”; “A compromise is acceptable if…” Optional hypothetical: suppose the second ship has much less time, or credible evidence shows one proposed penalty will increase future rebellion. Change one relevant detail; do not rewrite the historical outcome as fact.

Essential terms

  • Collective punishment: punishing a group without distinguishing each person’s responsibility.
  • Deterrence: discouraging an action through its expected costs.
  • Self-interest: concern for one’s own advantage.
  • Accountability: being answerable for decisions and their consequences.
  • Implementation: putting a decision into effect.

Fortnightly presentation: solo or pairs

Develop this Decision Note or the previous episode’s. Four slides, about 200–300 words of assessable speaker notes; 4–6 minutes including an audience question. Solo and pair work have equal status and workload. Pair members both contribute.

  1. Story and choice: one vivid image or dilemma; notes explain the historical context, fictional role and source.
  2. Principle and reasons: a short rule on the slide; notes compare benefits and costs of two responses to the same need, with a specific episode detail and fair opposing reason.
  3. Counterexample: a clearly labelled hypothetical changing one morally relevant detail; notes explain its challenge to the same rule.
  4. Revised or defended principle: justify retention or revision and acknowledge a limitation. Add the audience objection and response afterward.

Each student includes a labelled 40–60-word personal note on slide 4: an endorsed reason, what they reconsidered or defended, and their contribution. Submit a PowerPoint with notes intact or an accessible equivalent plus a notes export. Slides alone do not contain all evidence.

Teacher-only marking cues

Weekly note: completion with revision for relevant attempts at all five prompts and a specific detail. Grade neither moral agreement nor polished English. Keep writing separate from gameplay.

Presentation rubric: five criteria, each 0–2: (1) accurate case and successful comparison under 3b.3; (2) supported judgement; (3) fair alternative; (4) counterexample and justified response; (5) individual reasoning. 0 absent/mistaken; 1 partial; 2 clear and supported. First four may share a pair score; fifth is individual. Assess submitted notes, not presumed speaking confidence.

Two plausible paths: accepting a limited reversal to prevent immediate mass harm while keeping later objections explicit; demanding stronger individual safeguards despite risking coalition support. Ask what each gains, whom it exposes, and what evidence supports the comparison. Do not give these as finished student answers before submission.

Likely misconception: the highest score proves a universally right decision, or reversal meant a complete pardon. Feedback prompt: “Name the same need in both responses; identify who bears each cost; explain why your chosen tradeoff is defensible.”

Suitability: a clear urgent need, multiple defensible responses, concrete source details, explicit historical limits, seven consequential local choices, and a common recap across routes. No Canvas assignment or grades are posted by this package.
