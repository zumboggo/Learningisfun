# Shared illustrated background standard

All three classes use the same visual language as the manga vector cast: clean dark slate outlines, muted colours, restrained cel shadows and simplified forms. Keep faces and choices more visually prominent than scenery. Use eye-level views and a clear central foreground; avoid placing a desk, railing or other obstacle where characters' feet will appear. No people baked into the backdrop. Leave lettering out so prose remains the source of information.

The studio's **Illustrated background library** includes versioned classroom, writing-workshop and rift images. Selecting one embeds its WebP data into the draft, so future library changes cannot alter a published episode. Reference them with `scene classroom`, `scene workshop`, or `scene rift`. Historical settings still require appropriate location-specific imagery; do not reuse a modern classroom merely because the class is Ethics or World Literature.

## Repeatable image prompt

“Create a 16:9 visual-novel background of [source-grounded place and time]. No people or text. Match simple manga-inspired vector characters: clean dark slate outlines, flat muted colours, restrained two-tone cel shadows, simplified geometric scenery, no photorealistic surfaces or painterly texture. Eye-level camera, clear central foreground for standing characters, useful scenery at the edges. [Important historical/environmental details]. Keep the background less contrasted than the actors.”

Inspect at wide, medium and close framing and on phone before accepting. Preserve recognisable setting details when the camera zooms. Encode generated images as WebP and check the full episode's 5 MB limit. Do not overwrite published images; use a new versioned directory.
