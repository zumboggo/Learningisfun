## World Literature

Both sections: year-long study across cultures, textual evidence, inference, theme, language, heroism/duty/harmony/enlightenment, argument, research, and comparison. Read texts before class; use story circles, question formulation, debates, and analytical writing.

- **Unit 1 — Epic Tales and Timeless Voices:** Gilgamesh; Beowulf (Heaney); Odyssey, Iliad, Oedipus Rex selections; cultural definitions of heroism.
- **Unit 2 — Literary Mosaic:** Spanish and Latin American focus. Don Quixote (Cervantes), The Alchemist (Paulo Coelho), Borges's “The Circular Ruins” and “The South,” Monterroso's “The Eclipse.” Quest, identity, diction, syntax, and worldview.
- **Unit 3 — Echoes of the East:** Journey to the West: The Monkey King selections attributed to Wu Cheng’en; Shi Jing, Wang Wei, Basho, Buson, Issa, Korean sijo; duty, harmony, enlightenment, and the literary canon. Students write jueju, haiku, and sijo and curate an anthology.
- **Unit 4 — Speeches & Nonfiction:** language and power; speakers include Cicero, Churchill, Mandela, Gandhi, Malcolm X, Adichie, Malala, and Thunberg.

Do not label every Unit 2 author South American or every Unit 3 tradition interchangeable. Verify assigned translations, chapters, and source excerpts for each episode.
