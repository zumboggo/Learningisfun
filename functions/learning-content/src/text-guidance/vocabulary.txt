HOW A 10-QUESTION QUIZ DRAWS FROM THIS
###############################################################################

  A teaching week is thematically tight, so four of its six core cards share a
  family by design. That is right for teaching and wrong for a matching grid,
  where five near-identical backs stop being a vocabulary test. The two rules
  resolve if the question types split the work:

    Q1-5   MULTIPLE CHOICE, all five from THIS WEEK's new cards.
           Draw the distractors from the SAME family - siblings are what make
           a multiple-choice item discriminate.

    Q6-10  TWO MATCHING SETS, 5 points: ONE new card plus FOUR prior ones,
           each set spanning DIFFERENT families, max two per family.
           The two unmatched fronts should be same-family cards from an
           earlier week, so the distractors double as review.

  That lands exactly on the 60/40 split: 6 items new, 4 items prior.
  The confusable pairs never to place in one set are listed in
  AP_RHETORICAL_DEVICE_MAP.txt Part D5.
