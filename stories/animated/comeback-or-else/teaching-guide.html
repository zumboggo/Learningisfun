COMEBACK OR ELSE

AP Language · Week of October 19, 2026 · Seven review rounds, about 15–20 minutes plus four knowledge questions. Fictional K-pop studio, comic kidnapping and harmless foam-prop slapstick. Silent by default.

Purpose

Students identify a rhetorical choice with evidence and explain a plausible effect for a particular audience and purpose. A device name alone is insufficient. A sentence can contain several devices, and different audiences may respond differently.

Preference has no wrong answer. Students choose their favourite of three original lyrics, identify a foregrounded device, watch a short teacher-memory example, and apply the idea to their lyric. Mistakes lead to feedback and correction, not a failed adventure. Hidden practice counters only change supportive dialogue. They do not award grades.

Weekly connection

The saved AP W10 schedule lists answering objections: procatalepsis, concession, refutation, qualifier, untenable, specious. The finale asks students to acknowledge a reasonable criticism of repetition, then answer it with a specific audience purpose. It introduces anticipating that objection as procatalepsis. Suggested use

  1. Play individually or in pairs. Ask students to say a reason before choosing an explanation.
  2. At the debut, save the seven-line lyric sheet. Different tastes produce different sheets, not different moral or aesthetic grades.
  3. Optional two-minute transfer: choose one saved line and complete “The words ___ use ___, which may ___ for ___ because ___.” Then suggest an audience for whom it might fail.
  4. Optional objection practice: “Some might argue ___; I agree that ___, but in this context ___.” Keep the concession distinct from surrendering the whole claim.

Teacher checks

Common errors: treating any repetition as anaphora; treating all parallel phrasing as antithesis; assuming one line can only contain one device; calling every question hypophora; linking a sound to one inevitable emotion; confusing an absurd situation with verbal irony. In particular, Vex sincerely yelling “This is serious” while holding a duck is comic incongruity, not automatically verbal irony.

The four-question Didi fare checks the seven devices through paired distinctions and contextual analysis. Each question is 25 points; 75 completes the quiz, with explanations and retry. Optional writing remains separate. No Canvas assignment or class release is created by publication.
